# What listens, who can hear, and when

## What listens

The ears are one microphone capture on this Mac. They are off until you
turn them on, and you turn them on from the Settings page — the same
page this link sits on. While they are on, a red dot sits at the top of
every screen of Settings and Talk. While they are off, it doesn't.

They listen to `<microphone>`. "Follow the Mac" means the Mac's default
input, whatever that is right now. Name one input and they stay on it.

Your voice is turned into words by `<engine>`. When the engine is on this
Mac, your voice goes nowhere. When it is at another address, your voice
goes there.

## Who can hear

Your words go to the agents who are listening: `<names>`. Each one sends
what it hears to the service its model runs on; for a Claude agent, that
is Anthropic. An agent hears every line, not only the lines with its name
in them. Its name decides when it answers, not what it hears.

Any program running as you on this Mac can read the words too. The log
is yours, and readable by you.

Only people you approve can ring your agents. Each caller is tied to a
key.

## When

Never while you are on a call with your agents: the room's ear stops the
moment a call is live, and starts again when it ends. On a phone call or
FaceTime, the ears keep listening. Turn them off.

The ear sounds are a tick each time your words are caught. They play
only if the sounds service is installed and on.

## What is kept

Your words are kept in two places: a log on this Mac, one file a day;
and a mail for every finished turn, in the heard mailbox and in each
listening agent's own mailbox. Words you were still saying when a turn
ended are in the log only. An agent's own session transcript holds the
lines it heard as well.

Audio: `<kept>`. With recording on, one file is kept for every turn, in a
folder for each day — on this Mac yesterday, 791 files and 592 MB. With
recording off, no audio is kept. If a number of days is set, audio older
than that is deleted; with no number, it stays until you delete it.

There is no single place to delete your words. The log and the mailboxes
are files on this Mac, and you can delete them. Copies an agent already
sent to its model are not yours to delete.

## Who can change this

Anyone signed in to Settings with your passkey. Turn off is one tap on
any screen, and the ears stop within a second.
